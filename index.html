Which one should I pick?

Zapstore is an app store that keeps Myco up to date for you. Good if you'll use Myco often.

Download Myco puts the app straight on your phone. After it downloads:

  1. Tap the download when it finishes (or find it in Files › Downloads).
  2. If your phone asks, allow your browser to install apps. It's a one-time switch.
  3. Tap Install, then Open.

On first start Myco asks for Bluetooth, nearby devices and a VPN. The VPN stays on your phone — it's how Myco talks to other phones, not a route for your internet traffic. Keep Myco open on screen while you share; that's when phones can find each other.

How it works

One tap.
The app is yours.

Your friend opens the app they want to share. You tap your phones together, back to back. That's it — the app jumps straight from their phone to yours.

It opens like any other app, even with no signal. And now you can pass it on to the next person.

Game night

Play together.
Right here.

Multiplayer games that run phone to phone — no internet, no servers, just the people around you. One friend has the game; tap phones and you have it too.

Got a TV around? It can be the big screen.

So what is Myco?

Apps from the people next to you.

Usually, apps come from a store on the internet. With Myco, they come from a person — a friend, a teacher, whoever is running the event. They tap their phone to yours, and the app is on your phone too.

  • No internet? No problem.

    Phones talk to each other directly. Works in airplane mode.

  • Nothing to sign up for

    No account, no email, no password. Open it and go.

  • Pass it on

    Once you have an app, you can give it to the next person.

The mesh

Works where the internet doesn't.

In a park, on a festival field, in a basement hall, at a school with bad Wi-Fi. Phones close to each other link up directly and become a little network of their own — the mesh. Myco is your way into it.

  • Bluetooth
  • Wi-Fi Aware
  • Same Wi-Fi network
  • USB cable

Yours to keep

Feels like any other app.

Apps open full screen, and you can put them on your home screen. Chat with the people around you, tick off a shared checklist, play a game — all without a single bar of signal.

And each app only gets what you allow: who you are, or talking to the phones nearby. You decide.

Organize a group

Create a network where there is none.

A weekend in the mountains, a camp miles from the nearest mast. Before the signal drops, put the group's tools on one phone — the route, the packing list, a group chat — and tap them across to everyone. They hand them on to whoever they meet.

From then on the phones keep each other up to date. Tick something off the list and the phones in reach pick it up — even the one that catches up with the group later.

* Myco is in early development, do not rely on it for personal safety.

Works with no internet

  • Connecting, while both phones have Myco open
  • Handing an app to someone you're connected to
  • Opening any app already on your phone
  • Apps built to talk to nearby phones, while they're in reach

Needs the internet

  • Installing Myco itself
  • Adding an app nobody here has yet
  • Public Nostr content, like profiles and the AppStore feed
  • Anything an app loads from the web

Build for Myco

Build for the mesh.

Myco runs napplets: small web apps in a sandbox. Publish one to Nostr and people hand it on, phone to phone.

  • Just the web

    HTML, CSS and JavaScript. Any nsite runs offline too.

  • Talks to nearby phones

    Publish and read over the mesh. No server, no internet.

  • Asks, never takes

    Identity, relays, mesh, files: your app gets what the person grants.
